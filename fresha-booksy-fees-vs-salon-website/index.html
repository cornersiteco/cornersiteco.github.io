Fresha and Booksy fees: how your own website books new clients direct.

For salons, barbers, nail, lash and brow studios that book through Fresha or Booksy. Fees checked on their own pages, November 2026.

Keep your booking app. Just give new clients a front door that doesn't go through the marketplace.

The short answer

Fresha and Booksy are good booking tools, and both can bring you new clients through their marketplace. In exchange, they can charge a one-time fee for each brand-new client found that way. Both say bookings that come through your own links, like a Book button on your own website, don't carry that fee. A salon website gives the people who search your name on Google a direct way to book, with your prices, photos and reviews, and without your competitors listed next to you.

What the fees are (as of November 2026)

Fresha. Fresha's pricing page lists a 20% one-time commission, with a minimum of CA$6 per client, for brand-new clients who find you on the Fresha marketplace. Returning clients are free. Fresha's help centre says the fee applies only when all three are true: the client books through the Fresha marketplace, their email or phone number doesn't match anyone already in your client list, and they confirm it's their first visit. It also says the fee "never applies to returning clients and any bookings from your website, direct links, Instagram and Facebook". Fresha caps the fee on higher-value appointments and lists Canada among the countries where it's charged.

Booking through Fresha on Google or an AI assistant counts as the marketplace. In November 2026, Fresha launched its Partner Network, which shows your marketplace profile in Google Search, Google Maps and AI assistants like ChatGPT, where clients can book you without visiting Fresha. Fresha's help centre says the fee applies when a client "books through the Fresha Marketplace, including the Partner Network". So a brand-new client who books you through Fresha inside Google or an AI assistant can carry the same new-client fee.

One catch, in Fresha's own words: until December 31, 2026, the fee may still apply when a new client first discovered your marketplace profile and then booked through your website or a direct link. From January 1, 2027, Fresha says the fee will be based only on where the client books.

Booksy. On Booksy's pricing page, its Boost marketing feature is optional and free to turn on. While it's on, Booksy charges a one-time 30% fee on a Boost client's first visit, up to $100. Booksy says there's no commission on new clients who find you through your own marketing, or on any client when Boost is off. It also tells businesses to share their Booksy Profile Link so clients who book through it aren't charged. (The pricing page Booksy showed us is its US page, so check the amounts in your own account.)

Fees change. These are from each company's own pages, read November 25, 2026. Your monthly statement is the final word.

Where a website fits

  • People search your name. Someone hears about you from a friend, or sees your work on Instagram, then searches your salon's name on Google. Your website should be the result they tap, with a Book button that goes straight to your own booking link.
  • No competitors on the page. A marketplace page sits among other salons nearby. Your website shows only you.
  • Your prices, photos and reviews, the way you want them. Not squeezed into the app's layout.
  • You can switch apps. If you move from Booksy to Fresha, or to any other booking page, your website stays the same: you only change where the button points.

Put the same link in your Google Business Profile and your Instagram bio, so every place people find you leads to your own front door.

Get the link right

This is the part that saves the fee, so it's worth two minutes:

  1. Fresha: use the booking link Fresha gives you for your own website and social media (Fresha's pricing page calls them direct booking links), not the address of your marketplace listing. Fresha's partner terms say it's your job to use the correct link, and that Fresha won't refund a fee charged because the wrong one was used.
  2. Booksy: use your Booksy Profile Link, the one Booksy tells you to share with clients.
  3. Check it's working. In Fresha, marketplace clients carry a "New Fresha Client" label, and each fee shows in your Business Wallet and monthly invoice. If a booking from your website shows up as a marketplace client, look at that client's journey first, then contact Fresha support.

Questions

Do I have to leave Fresha or Booksy?

No. Keep the app you use for your calendar, reminders and payments. Your website's Book button simply opens your own booking link in that app, so nothing changes for you or your team.

Will a website stop every new-client fee?

No. Clients who book you through the Fresha marketplace, including Fresha's booking inside Google or AI assistants, or through Booksy Boost while it's on, can still be charged as new clients. A website gives the people who search your name a direct way to book. Fresha also says that until December 31, 2026 its fee may apply when a new client first saw your marketplace profile and then booked through your website or a direct link. From January 1, 2027, Fresha says the fee will depend only on where the client books.

Isn't my Instagram link enough?

Fresha says bookings from Instagram and Facebook don't carry its new-client fee either, so keep that link. A website adds what Instagram can't: you can show up when people search your name or "nail salon near me" on Google, with your prices, hours, map and reviews on one page you own.
